Answers are written to apply generally: no local details (a state, a single glacier, a named person) unless the
claim can't be answered without them. Covered claims get extra search words (data-also) instead of a new answer.

- 2026-11-01, from X: "Show me repeatable experimental proof that doubling CO2 raises air temperature 2.7 °F (1.5 °C)."
  Extend no-experiment with a climate-sensitivity bullet (+ search words: doubling double co2 climate sensitivity 2.7 F
  how much warming per doubling repeatable). Measured part: forcing per doubling ~3.9 W/m2 (AR6, from lab spectra);
  without feedbacks that alone gives ~1.2 °C (basic radiation physics). Estimated part (label as estimate): with
  feedbacks AR6 best estimate 3 °C, likely 2.5-4 °C, from three independent lines (physics of feedbacks, the measured
  record since 1850, past climates). The planet can't go in a lab; the "repeat" is old predictions checked against
  what happened (link models.html past predictions).
- 2026-11-01, from X (on a 130,000-year GHG forcing chart): "A splice of dodgy proxies with modern direct
  measurements." New entry, e.g. id proxy-splice (search words: splice spliced proxies proxy dodgy ice core bubbles
  hockey stick grafted mike's nature trick apples and oranges). Points: gas concentrations from ice cores are not a
  proxy, they are samples of ancient air trapped in bubbles, measured the same way as modern air. Law Dome cores
  overlap Mauna Loa (1958 onward) and agree within a few ppm (Etheridge et al. 1996; MacFarling Meure et al. 2006);
  firn air bridges the gap. Caveat to state honestly: older/slow-snowfall cores smooth out short swings (decades to
  centuries), but a rise as big as today's would still show. Temperature proxies are a different question: link
  medieval-warm. Link co2.html#hbox.
  Also (2026-11-01, X): "Invented data, impossible to know W/m2 a century ago." Forcing isn't measured in the past;
  it's calculated from measured gas concentrations with lab-measured absorption physics, the same calculation
  confirmed today by direct measurements (Feldman et al. 2015, surface CO2 forcing rising ~0.2 W/m2 per decade at two
  US sites; Harries et al. 2001, satellite spectra). Search words: invented impossible to know watts per square meter
  forcing 100 years ago calculated.
- 2026-11-01, from X: "Then why isn't it 10 degrees hotter? The temperature data doesn't support the forcing."
  Bullet for how-much-human (+ search words: why not hotter 10 degrees forcing doesn't match temperature too little
  warming pipeline ocean lag). Rough check (label as such): GHG forcing ~4.2 W/m2 minus aerosol cooling -> net ~3.1
  W/m2 (IGCC 2025); ~1.1 W/m2 of that is still going into the ocean (EEI), so ~2 W/m2 has been 'answered' by
  warming; at ~0.77 °C per W/m2 (ECS 3 °C / 3.9 W/m2, estimate) that is ~1.5 °C, close to the observed 1.37 °C
  human-induced. Nobody projected 10 °C; that would need roughly 13 W/m2. Link eei.html, too-late.
- 2026-11-01, from X: "Temperature leads, CO2 follows. Known among scientists for 150 years." New entry, e.g. id
  co2-lags (search words: lag lags leads follows temperature first 800 years ice core vostok co2 follows temperature
  chicken egg). Points: in ice ages, an orbital nudge started the warming, the warming ocean released CO2, and CO2
  then amplified and spread the warming worldwide — both cause and effect (feedback). Antarctic lag at the end of ice
  ages: ~800 years in early studies (Caillon et al. 2003), near-synchronous within a few centuries in better-dated ones
  (Parrenin et al. 2013); for global temperature, CO2 came first at the last deglaciation (Shakun et al. 2012). Today
  CO2 clearly leads: its rise is from fossil fuels (link co2-not-ours) and preceded the warming. "150 years" is wrong:
  the lag was found in ice cores ~1999-2003; what's been known since 1859-1896 (Tyndall, Arrhenius) is that CO2 warms.
  Analogy: credit card interest — debt causes interest, interest adds debt.
